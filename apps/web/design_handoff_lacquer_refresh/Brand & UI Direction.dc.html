UI & UX recommendations

RANKED BY IMPACT
1

Give the app one signature surface

Every screen today is white cards on grey — the interface has no memorable moment. A deep aubergine header block on Today, on the points total and on the Copilot bar becomes the thing users picture when they think of the product, and it costs nothing in legibility.

2

"Do today" must never be empty

The home screen currently says "nothing urgent today" while two deadlines sit in the tile above it. Surface the real next actions with the deadline, the dollar figure and a concrete suggestion — an action card should tell the user what to do, not just that something exists.

3

Collapse the balance list into the total

Three full-width rows for three programs is a lot of screen for three numbers. Folded into the total card as a stacked bar plus legend, the split reads instantly and the fold moves destinations — the emotional payoff — above it.

4

Show the gap, not just the goal

Destinations you can't afford yet are the strongest retention hook in the product. Keep them visible with a progress bar and "49,800 short" instead of hiding them — that is the reason to come back next month.

5

Show your reasoning

"Safe to apply now" asks for trust without earning it. Three lines of evidence — eligibility, whether the min spend is realistic at their actual rate, net value after the fee — turn a recommendation into an argument, and net dollars beat raw points as the headline for a decision.

6

One control set per list, not four

Next Card stacks a summary row, a preferences row, a hidden-cards row, three count tiles, two dropdowns, a view toggle and a chip row before the first result. Chips carry the counts and the filter; sort and view move into the header. Ineligible cards stay in the list, dimmed, with the reason — a filtered-out card teaches nothing.

7

Turn the card record into a pace check

Approval date, expiry, fee and fee-charge date is a database row. What the user needs is whether they will make the min spend: progress, required daily rate, and their actual rate. Move the reference data behind a Details disclosure.

8

Copilot as a bar, and one FAB

A 170px mic button treats voice as the default when typing is the more common input, and the radial menu duplicates it a second time. A persistent input bar with the mic on the right handles both, and the mascot shrinks to the Copilot avatar so it stops covering content on every screen.

OPEN QUESTIONS

Three calls I made without you: the product wordmark is "Copilot" (taken from your assistant and the phcopilot.app domain), the mascot is reduced to a small avatar rather than removed, and Deals is folded out of the tab bar to make room for Today. Say the word on any of these and I will rework. If you would rather see a different palette entirely — ink navy or oxblood on the same structure — that is a quick second option.
